    How the planner works

    • Ions: ions across the three sites. The common ones (Cs, MA, FA; Pb, Sn; I, Br, Cl) are always shown; use the "Add" menus for the rest. Device counts in the menus are compositions in the Perovskite Database that include the ion.
    • Band gap: for the eight common ions, a quadratic ridge regression on their site fractions, trained on the median band gap reported for each of compositions (5-fold cross-validated error about eV). For any other ion, a model that describes ions by their properties (electronegativity, row, lone pair, charge, size, empty sites) instead of their names: a straight-line halide trend plus the three most similar published compositions. Tested by hiding every composition containing an ion and predicting them, its error is about 0.03 eV close to known compositions and about 0.2 eV farther away; compositions unlike anything in the data get no estimate. Most gaps in the database were assigned from the composition rather than measured, so both models reflect the literature's consensus.
    • Structure: the charges must add up to zero, so with +1 cations and −1 anions the B-site must average +2. A +3 or +4 metal can still work three ways, which the planner offers as one-click fixes: pair it with a +1 metal (a double perovskite), leave some B-sites empty (a vacancy-ordered compound such as MA₃Bi₂I₉ or Cs₂SnI₆), or swap some halide for a −2 chalcogenide (a chalcohalide). Goldschmidt tolerance factor and octahedral factor use site-averaged radii. "Fits" means 0.86–0.98, "near the edge" means 0.80–0.86 or 0.98–1.06. Radii: Shannon (1976) for inorganic ions (Sn²⁺ 1.10 Å is an estimate); Kieslich, Sun & Cheetham (2014, 2015) for molecular ions. Ions without a tabulated radius, and empty sites, are left out of the factors.
    • Evidence: distance between site-fraction vectors; "close" means within 0.10. Related compounds that use the same ions in a different structure (such as MA₃Bi₂I₉) are listed but not counted. Top 5% is used instead of the single best value because the database contains a few impossible entries. Additive counts are approximate text matches.
    • Lifetime: Kaplan–Meier median hours until efficiency falls to 80%, grouped by the B-site metals and the test atmosphere, counting devices that outlasted their tests. A median is shown only for groups with at least 10 tests.
    • Raw materials: 500 nm film, pseudo-cubic lattice set by the X-site radius (fitted to 6.31, 5.93 and 5.68 Å for iodide, bromide and chloride). Prices and producer shares: USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries 2026, at the grade USGS reports. Cesium and rubidium have no commodity price; their research-grade prices are shown separately. Organic cations and abundant elements are not priced.
    • Out of scope: additives, 2D layered phases, oxides and other all-chalcogenide perovskites, and device-specific efficiency or lifetime predictions.
